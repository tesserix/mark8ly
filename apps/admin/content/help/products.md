Products are the heart of your store. Mark8ly's product model supports variants, rich media, categorization, and bulk operations so you can manage anything from a five-SKU shop to a catalog of thousands.

## Creating products

Open **Products** in the sidebar and click **Add product**. Every product needs:

- A clear, descriptive **title** (it appears in search results, social shares, and your storefront catalog).
- At least one **variant** with a price.

Write descriptions that help customers make purchasing decisions — focus on materials, dimensions, use cases, and what makes the product different. A good description reduces returns and support questions; generic filler text helps no one.

## Variants and options

Products can have multiple variants representing different configurations like size, color, or material.

1. Define your **options** first — for example, `Size` with values `S, M, L, XL`.
2. Mark8ly generates the corresponding variant matrix automatically.
3. Fill in the per-variant details.

Each variant has its own:

- **SKU**
- **Price** and **compare-at price**
- **Cost price** (for profitability tracking)
- **Inventory quantity**
- **Weight** (used by weight-based shipping)

> Use a meaningful SKU convention like `TEE-BLK-M`. Your team can identify items at a glance during packing, and reports become much easier to read.

## Product media

Mark8ly accepts JPEG, PNG, and WebP. The first image becomes the primary display image on your storefront — drag images to reorder them.

Images that convert well typically include:

- A clean front shot
- A back or side angle
- Detail shots (texture, stitching, branding)
- Lifestyle context showing the product in use

Customers who can see a product clearly from different perspectives convert at higher rates. Use the image cropping tool to keep aspect ratios consistent across your catalog.

## Categories and organization

Organize products into categories to help customers browse. Categories appear in your storefront navigation and filter options.

- Each product can belong to **one primary category** and any number of **secondary categories**.
- Keep your category tree shallow — ideally two levels deep at most.

> Deeply nested categories confuse customers. If you have a large catalog, use **tags** for flexible cross-cutting organization in addition to categories.

## Status and publishing

Products have three statuses:

| Status | Visible to customers? | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Draft** | No | While you're still building the listing. |
| **Active** | Yes | Listing is complete and inventory is sufficient. |
| **Archived** | No | Permanently discontinued — preserved for order-history reference. |

Always **archive** rather than **delete** discontinued products. Archived products preserve the references on past orders, so historical reports and reorders stay accurate.

## Bulk operations

For larger catalogs, select multiple products from the product list and apply bulk actions:

- Change status (e.g. activate a seasonal collection at once)
- Update categories
- Adjust pricing

You can also export your product catalog as **CSV** for offline editing, migrations from another platform, or sweeping price adjustments. Re-import the CSV to apply your changes back to Mark8ly.
